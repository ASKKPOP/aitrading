Swing Signal Stack

Finding 3–5 day (extendable to ~2 weeks) buy and sell points in US stocks and crypto by combining crowd psychology, the macro tide, changes in a company's fundamental story, and what informed money is doing.

⚠️ Research framework, not financial advice — see the disclaimer at the end. Implementation and last-month test: docs/reports/swing-signal-stack-ship-20260831.html.

01 · Core rule

Two axes must agree

Every reliable signal needs agreement between two independent axes: the quantitative (price, volume, technicals — RSI, trend structure, relative strength) and the qualitative (sentiment, news, fundamentals/expectations, crowd psychology). Sentiment alone is noisy and manipulable; price alone tells you what happened, not why. When they agree → act. When they conflict → WAIT — on that ticker, at that moment.

Sentiment bearishSentiment bullish
Price bullishWAIT — price firm, crowd fearful → watch for contrarian entryBUY zone
Price bearishSELL / avoidWAIT — crowd euphoric, price weak → possible trap / top

What “WAIT” actually means

  • It applies per ticker, per moment — across a 10–20 name watchlist, some names agree on most days.
  • Conflict is temporary: write the resolving condition (“price reclaims the 5-day high on volume”), set an alert, and do nothing until it fires. That is active waiting.
  • Being flat is a position — and the retail trader's only structural edge (no one forces you to be invested).
  • If the system says WAIT on everything for two weeks, the regime is choppy and cash was the right trade.

03 · The four pillars

Engine, human factor, tide, informed money

Pillar 4 — Fundamental catalysts (the engine)

Great traders trade a company's future — but on a swing horizon “future” only moves price when expectations change: an earnings surprise, raised or cut guidance, estimate revisions, a product event. Everyone already knows NVIDIA is a great company — that is in the price. The tradeable moment is when the market's view of the future shifts. Measure: EPS/revenue surprise % vs consensus · guidance direction · estimate-revision momentum · days to next catalyst. It sets the watchlist, the direction, and the trade window (3–5 days normally; 1–2 weeks when the catalyst is dated further out). Caveat: “good company” ≠ buy signal — story stocks are where crowds overpay for exciting futures, and the two-axis rule matters most there.

Pillar 1 — Social & news sentiment (human factor)

The signal is not the sentiment level — it's the rate of change: a mention surge with a tone flip usually leads price by hours to days. Measure: mentions/hour vs the 7-day baseline · bullish/bearish ratio · headline impact. Caveat: sentiment lags fast moves, gets gamed by bots and pump rooms, and peaks at tops. Never trade it without the quant axis agreeing.

Pillar 2 — Macro regime (the tide)

Tech and growth stocks are long-duration assets: they move inversely to real yields and with liquidity. Measure: 10Y yield direction · DXY · VIX · Fed-speak tone · the event calendar (CPI, FOMC, PCE, jobs, big-tech earnings). Rule: no fresh swing position < 24h before a binary macro event unless the trade is the event. Caveat: geopolitical headlines hit tech supply chains fastest and override technicals.

Pillar 3 — Smart-money tracking

Three tiers: institutional 13F, insider Form 4 buys, congress disclosures. The strongest signal is cluster agreement — several independent actors accumulating the same name in the same quarter. Use it for the watchlist (which tickers), not timing (when): 13F and congress data lag up to 45 days, and the position may already be exited.

08 · Crypto adaptation

Same stack, three feeds swapped

Crypto is more psychology-driven than stocks (no earnings anchor). The two-axis rule, psychology table, macro score, and risk gate apply unchanged. Three feeds get replaced — one swap is a strict upgrade.

Rules that tighten for crypto: 24/7 market → time stop in clock hours (e.g. 120h), shocks land on thin-liquidity weekends; volatility several times higher → ≤1% sizing matters more; manipulation (pump groups, wash-traded volume, botted sentiment) far worse → never trade sentiment without quant confirmation; risk of total loss is real.

10 · The minimum loop

Manual, under an hour a day

  1. Build the watchlist weekly (Pillars 3+4). Scan Dataroma / Quiver / OpenInsider for cluster buys; add names with upward estimate revisions. 10–20 tickers max.
  2. Know every catalyst date (Pillar 4). Next earnings, next product event per name → decides the trade window and the blackout rule.
  3. Score the macro regime each morning (Pillar 2). 10Y direction, VIX, DXY, today's calendar → one number, −100…+100. No fresh entries within 24h of a binary event.
  4. Scan sentiment per watchlist name (Pillar 1). Mention surge vs baseline? Tone flip? Fear/greed extreme? Score it.
  5. Demand technical confirmation. Composite ≥ +40 means nothing until price confirms — level reclaimed, relative strength, volume behind the move.
  6. Write the exit before the entry. Stop, target, and time stop (D+5, or catalyst date + 1–2 days), on paper, before the order.
  7. Size to survive. Risk a fixed small fraction (≤1% of account) per trade. Signal quality picks direction; sizing decides survival.
  8. Log every trade, backtest every rule. The weights above are guesses until your own data says otherwise.

Automate the step that hurts most first, one at a time.

⚠️ Research aid, not financial advice. Signals are probabilistic and can be wrong. Sentiment indicators lag, are noisy, and are manipulable (bots, pump rooms, fake news). 13F and congress data arrive with up to a 45-day delay. Stock and crypto trading carry risk of substantial or total loss. Use hard stop-losses, position limits, and a max-drawdown circuit breaker — final decisions and outcomes are your own responsibility.
