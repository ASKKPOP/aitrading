The Automation page — your first visit

The Automation page is mission control for your AI trading: it is where you start an AI-run TRADING, watch it work, and stop it. This guide walks the page top to bottom — what each control is, how to use it, what happens after you press start, and where to look while it runs.

Three things to know before anything else

① Everything you start here trades a simulated wallet by default — practice money, not yours. ② The AI works on the server, around the clock; you can close the tab and it keeps going. ③ You always keep the stop button — every TRADING, and every fleet, can be paused or stopped in one or two clicks.

2 · The account switch — Simulator or Real

The very first row of the page is a two-way switch:

  • ◐ Simulator — a paper wallet. You practice with virtual funds; no real money is ever used. This is the default, and the right place to spend your first days.
  • ● Real — connects to a real exchange account. If you have not connected one yet, pressing Real takes you to the Accounts page to link a broker first. Once connected, the page shows connected gateway: (your broker).

Everything described in this guide — the TRADINGs, the fleet, the profit numbers — runs on the simulated wallet unless you have deliberately connected a gateway and accepted the live-trading terms. A rule or run that would use real money is always marked with a red ⚠ REAL MONEY badge, so it can never look like a simulation at a glance.

3 · The summary strip — your money, at a glance

Four tiles in the header answer "how is it going?" without opening anything. The period chips — Today · This week · This month · This year — re-window the numbers; the default is This week.

Two numbers deserve a word of explanation:

  • Unrealized is always "now". Whatever period chip you pick, the unrealized part is the mark-to-market of the positions open at this moment.
  • Why "current exit rails"? The exit safety structure (position-scaled stops plus a trailing profit lock) was replaced on 2026-07-25. The 🛡️ tile isolates results earned under the structure that actually protects your positions today, so one old number cannot masquerade as current performance.

Under the tiles sits 🧠 AI Analysis (30 min) — covered in section 7. It is the answer to "what did the AI do while I was away?"

4 · Starting your first TRADING

A TRADING is one AI-run strategy bound to one symbol: the AI re-analyses it on a schedule and trades when its decision clears your thresholds. There are four ways to start one, sorted here from least effort to most control. Which ones you see depends on your plan (section 9).

Which path fits you?
All four end in the same place: a TRADING card in the grid below

⓪ Agent (1 symbol) — set only a budget

Press ➕ Make by New Trading and the creation dialog opens on the mode chips. In ⓪ there is exactly one required input: Total budget ($), from $100. The AI picks a promising symbol, creates the TRADING, and journals its reasoning every cycle. One position never uses more than 25% of the budget. The footnote on the button is worth repeating: the kill switch is the TRADING list below — stop it there and it halts instantly, and the risk gate, breakers and budget cap cannot be bypassed by the AI.

① Simple — pick a coin and a risk level

Two choices instead of one: the symbol (the live top-5 by volume, or 🔍 AI auto-pick, where a scanner chooses from volume surges and new listings each cycle) and a risk card:

The exit numbers on each card are what the server will actually enforce for that level, shown before you commit. Below the cards, plans with futures access also choose the book type: Spot (fully funded, long-only, no liquidation) or Futures (the desk can also short, with 1–10× isolated-margin leverage — at N× the position liquidates on a 100/N% adverse move).

② Full Auto — launch a fleet

One budget, many TRADINGs. You set the number of TRADINGs (up to your plan's cap), the total budget, an optional profit goal % (reach it and the fleet banks everything, then restarts itself; 0 means run until you stop it), a profit level (Balance / High Profit / Risk & High Profit — progressively larger position sizing and automatic leverage), the markets it may trade (crypto, US stocks, futures, forex — or all), and a leverage policy (Auto lets the risk level decide). Press 🚀 Launch fleet and the AI researches the markets, composes N TRADINGs, and keeps the roster filled as members finish. Monitoring a fleet is section 8.

🧠 Make by AI Analyst — see one analysis first

The second header button runs the full four-analyst desk once, right now — News, Sentiment, Fundamentals & Macro, and Technical each write a brief; a trader and a risk reviewer debate them into a decision. You watch the pipeline live, then get the decision (BUY / SELL / HOLD), its confidence, a written report, a decision detail (thesis alignment, momentum, entry quality, risk context, verification), and the analyst briefs. No order is placed. If you like what you read, ▶ Start auto-trading with this turns it into a TRADING with balanced defaults in one click. It is research only — not financial advice.

If a start is refused. Starting can be blocked by a resolvable conflict — for example an open position or an old auto-executing rule on the same symbol. The TRADING is still created as a draft in the grid, and its Start button opens a dialog that names the conflict and offers to resolve it for you ("Close & disable, then start"). Nothing fails silently.

5 · What happens after you press start

You do not need to keep the page open. The engine on the server picks your TRADING up within seconds and runs it on its own clock.

The life of a TRADING
The cycle repeats at your chosen interval; the safety watchers never sleep

A cycle that ends in "skipped" is the system working, not failing: the decision did not clear your confidence bar, the market was closed, or a safety check refused the order — and the reason is written into the event log every time.

Your first hour — what is normal

  • "No position — holding cash" is normal. The AI only enters when a decision clears your confidence threshold and every safety check. Hours without a fill are common, especially on Stable settings.
  • Skipped rows are normal. Expect decision, order design and skipped entries in the event log long before the first trade.
  • A tiny negative Profit is normal right after a first entry: an open position carries its entry fee until it closes.
  • Fleet members start staggered. Each TRADING's cycle is offset a few minutes from its siblings so they do not all fire at once.

6 · Monitoring a TRADING — the grid and the drawer

Each card in 📈 AI Automation Master is one TRADING. The card answers "is it alive and what is it holding?"; the drawer behind it answers "what exactly has it done, and why?"

Where to look for what
Five questions, five surfaces — all on this one page

Reading a card

  • Status line — Ready (a draft, not started), Waiting (armed, next cycle scheduled), Running (pulsing dot), Paused, Stopping, Stopped (with the reason). A (?) on the card opens "How to read a TRADING card", which explains every field in place.
  • Capability chip — 🟡 Market closed means it resumes at the next session; 🔴 Cannot trade means the settings make trading permanently impossible (for example a target move too small to ever cover fees). When 🔴 cards hold capital, a banner above the grid counts them and offers Release all to free the money in one click.
  • The four facts — Max bet · Interval (with the confidence bar, like 45m·≥50%) · Type (spot, or futures with its leverage) · which AI runs it.
  • The live book — either "No position — holding cash" or the position: LONG/SHORT, quantity, entry, current mark, unrealized and realized P&L, and for futures the leverage, collateral, liquidation price and the liquidation-defense tiers protecting it.
  • The activity line rotates every few seconds between what the TRADING is doing now and the countdown to its next cycle; an arriving event flashes in with a ⚡.
  • ⧗ data age — how old the last price is. Markets that update by polling refresh on an interval; the badge is the honest measure of staleness.

The detail drawer

Click a card and a drawer slides in with the full picture: a P&L header (realized, trades, current mark), the equity sparkline, the lifecycle buttons, and the event log — the newest 60 entries of decision, trade, skipped, order design, order intent, status, equity, alert and more. Skips name their stage and reason (for example fee edge, risk/reward floor, budget, confidence rank) — this log is the first place to look whenever you wonder why nothing happened.

Auto-restart

When a TRADING stops because it reached its own goal — profit target hit, or its trade limit — a new TRADING with the same settings takes over automatically, and the event log records "Restarted as TRADING #N". Stopping it yourself, of course, does not restart anything.

7 · 🧠 AI Analysis (30 min) — the desk's diary

The button under the summary tiles opens a review of everything the AI recorded, folded into 30-minute cycles — including the hours you were not signed in. Pick a day with the date arrows; each row is one cycle: either "nothing moved", or a one-line tally (decisions · skipped · opened · closed) with the realized result. Expand a cycle and you get, per TRADING: the action it chose with its confidence, the desk's written report, any status transitions with reasons, and what was skipped and why. If you check your fleet once a day, this is the screen to check.

8 · Monitoring a fleet — 🚀 Full Auto live monitor

While a fleet is live, its monitor sits above the grid:

  • 🎯 Fleet index — the whole fleet as one number, rebased to 100 at launch. 103.42 means the fleet is up 3.42% on its budget; the card also shows the P&L in dollars and the launch date. The (?) explains exactly how it is computed.
  • Status line — the fleet state and its reason in plain words (Built 4/5, Replenishing (2/5), Drawdown warning — new entries paused…), the marked equity, and the two rails: at −warn% new entries pause ⚠︎, at −stop% everything is closed ⏹. If the warning rail fires, a strip appears with Resume trading (your responsibility).
  • Member chips — one per TRADING, with a pulsing dot while it runs and its marked P&L; a chip flashes green when something changed. Click a chip to open that TRADING's drawer. A toggle shows or hides finished members.
  • Trading activity tab — the recent fills as a table (type, symbol, amount, price, P&L, date), filterable by market.
  • Lifecycle — Pause stops new entries only (open positions keep their stop-loss/take-profit protection; Resume continues). ⏹ Stop all (flatten) halts the fleet and closes every position immediately — a stopped fleet cannot be restarted, you launch a new one.
  • Edit fleet — change the profit goal and the slot count while it runs. As you type, the panel shows the dollar target and per-slot budget live, along with the 🤖 AI-recommended maximum slot count for your budget. Growing adds slots as budget headroom frees up; shrinking drains — excess slots retire only when their positions finish on their own, and nothing is ever force-closed by a resize.
