04

What was refuted

Every change carried a pre-registered condition that could kill it. One did.

F–ARM — refuted in two hours

Two outcome agents identified the breakeven ratchet as the loss engine: it arms at a flat 0.160% of notional regardless of the bracket the desk authored, so it banked ~+0.035% while losers ran the full stop. The fix scaled the arm to the target. Then:

FIL run 2814, SHORT, $93.73 notional
  peak unrealized      +$0.31060
  OLD arm (0.160%)      $0.1500  -> WOULD have armed
  NEW arm (0.30 x 5%)   $1.4060  -> did NOT arm
  realized                        -$1.90163

One trade, essentially the entire −$2.03 the window gave up. Zero ratchet fires in those two hours against eight in the two before — the higher arm did not make the ratchet fire better, it stopped it firing. Reverted.

The methodological error, named so it is not repeated. My counterfactual sampled ratchet fires that had already banked — a population that structurally cannot contain the failure “position never arms and runs to its stop.” Adversarial review replayed a window containing losers and predicted this exact outcome before deploy. A counterfactual validated only on winners is not a counterfactual. “The arm is too low” and “raising it helps” are different claims; only the first had evidence.

Four of my own measurements were wrong today and each was caught before it drove a decision: a vacuous 1-minute window reported as “holding”; a T-vs-space timestamp comparison that silently matched every row of the day; an entry field mistaken for the live mark, which made 13.8% of decisions look in-zone; and an F–EXIT check that counted any proposed sell as an exit without checking the position sign.

05

What is left

Ordered by what the evidence says costs money, not by how easy it is to fix.

P&L

The ratchet is still scale-blind

The defect is real and unfixed: a flat 0.160% arm that ignores the authored bracket, banking fee-scale gains against stop-scale losses. My fix for it was refuted. Re-enable only with a replay on a window containing losers. The code path and its tests remain; RATCHET_ARM_TARGET_FRAC is the knob.

P&L

Fees exceed profit by 5×

$4.40 paid against $0.87 realized. The median trade moves about 1.45× the fee wall, so the edge is roughly nothing. Three re-entries within minutes of an exit all lost. A trailing-lock close also stops the run, so every trade burns a slot and forces a rebuild — 20 runs rebuilt in 100 minutes at one point.

quality

The desk trades against its own written plan

12 of 13 early fills were taken outside or against the entry condition the desk itself had just published; several cited a “listing” or “upgrade” appearing in no analyst brief. The plan_adherence field self-reported “followed” and was wrong every time it did.

safety

A rule fallback can cancel a stop fail-safe

Verified: a run dispatched by a stop hit answered with decided_by: rule and “decision json unparseable”, and stop_router.clear has no decided_by guard — so a non-decision consumed the ruling and cancelled the 180-second fail-safe. Third door into a family that has already cost real money twice. Not shipped; fix recorded.

safety

A mixed sign-and-unit slip still floors the stop

The unit-slip recovery requires every percentage field to be sub-floor. One live run authored stop_pct 0.03 with target_pct -0.05, failed that test, and got 0.30% instead of 3.0% — 10× tighter than authored. Predicted by review before deploy, then observed live.

watch

The legacy trail carries the same blindness

It activates at a flat 0.400% of notional. Whatever replaces the ratchet fix must account for it, or the binding constraint simply moves rather than disappears.

06

How this was worked

Every change carried a falsifier registered before deploy, stating what result would kill it. Every ship went through adversarial review plus an independent verifier, and every test was mutation-checked — a pin that survives its own mutation is not a pin.

That process earned its keep repeatedly. Review caught a 16× sizing hole, a size_frac: 0 back-door veto that would have made a ship look successful while trading nothing, an exit-sizing inversion and its mirror on the short side, and a re-armed fail-safe hazard — all before production. Two mutations survived a first pass and were closed.

The recurring failure worth carrying forward: tests that look behavioural and are not — source-text pins, re-implementation pins, and unit tests that never exercise the wiring. Five instances in two days. Dropping one column from a SQL query would have made an entire fix inert in production with no test failing.
