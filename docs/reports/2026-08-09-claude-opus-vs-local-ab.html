What the numbers actually say

  • Fewer, bigger trades. Opus traded half as often (66 vs 138) and won less often (60.8% vs 69.3%), but its average win was 3× larger (+0.156 vs +0.052) and its gain/loss ratio nearly doubled (2.16 vs 1.33). That is the mandate's shape — "fewer candidates you would actually pay to hold" — and it is genuinely present.
  • But the whole edge is one trend. KAITO alone paid +3.12 across four runs (three trailing-lock wins +1.32/+2.15/+0.84, one over-extended re-entry −1.19). That single symbol is larger than the entire window's net. Opus-excluding-KAITO = −0.52.
  • The windows are not comparable. Local ran mostly on a weekday tape (stocks, futures, forex all open — more, higher-quality opportunities). Opus ran mostly on a weekend (crypto only, thin, choppy). This confounds the comparison in both directions: Opus got a lucky trend, but a worse overall tape.
  • Sample is tiny. 23 hours, one weekend, ~50 closes. Not remotely enough to separate skill from variance.

The honest bottom line

Opus is not proven better on this data, and it would be dishonest to say otherwise. What is true: (1) the win-shape under Opus is the one the mandate wants — bigger winners, tighter losers; (2) that shape this window came almost entirely from riding one crypto trend, not from a broad edge; (3) the fair test is a full weekday window where stocks and futures are open, which starts Monday.

Recommended: keep Opus on through at least one weekday session and re-cut this same table. If the ex-KAITO (ex-single-trend) net turns positive on a normal tape, that is real evidence. Until then the +2.61 headline should be read as "+one trend, otherwise flat-to-slightly-negative."

Method & caveats

  • Net uses the fee-accurate ledger: each fill contributes its realized P&L, or minus its fee on an opening leg (COALESCE(pnl, −COALESCE(fee_usd,0))).
  • Window boundary is the Settings-page switch timestamp (2026-08-08 08:43Z). Decision events since then carry llm:{provider:anthropic, model:claude-opus-4-8} — attribution is by stamp, not wall clock.
  • Platform daemon LLM calls (hot-issue, etc.) run on their own routing and are not part of this comparison.
  • Trailing-lock and the exit rails are model-agnostic (they are code); what changed between windows is the desk's decisions, not its execution.
