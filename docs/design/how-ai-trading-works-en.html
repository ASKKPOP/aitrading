1. The 30-minute loop, end to end

Every automation run is one AI trading desk assigned to one symbol on one book. The worker wakes it on its own interval (typically 30 minutes), and one cycle flows through nine stations. Only one of them is an LLM; everything else is deterministic machinery that feeds it, rails it, and journals it.

2. Who authors what — AI vs machine

The design rule of the whole platform: every trading decision belongs to the sooppiy.com AI; the machine's job is to bound it, compile it, and record it. Plan 17 moved the last load-bearing numbers from the machine column into the AI column.

3. The Order Intent and the basis switch

The desk answers with one JSON object. The load-bearing idea is that size_frac means a fraction of different things depending on what the desk is doing — and the platform, not the model, decides which:

The desk's order, field by field

One decision, as your AI desk actually sends it every field journalled
intent
reduce
What I want to do. One word from a fixed menu of seven — here, "bank part of my position". The menu is the whole vocabulary; the desk cannot invent an eighth verb.
size_frac
0.5
How much. Half — of the position it holds, because this is an exit. The platform, never the model, decides what the fraction is measured against: exits measure against the book, entries against the max bet.
arm
none this cycle
A standing trigger, optional. "If price rises above 27,800, bank half without waiting for my next cycle." The desk names the level; the machine watches it every minute and merely obeys. An arm can only ever shed risk.
why
"bank half into strength; thesis intact but extended"
One sentence, required. The reason is recorded next to the order, so you can always read back why your desk did what it did.
The full menu: openadd reduceclose flip holdarm
takes risk — may be shrunk or refused sheds risk — never refused reverses / acts later

4. The asymmetric rails and the compiler

The ledger only ever executes four verbs — buy | sell | short | cover — and that does not change. Intents compile to them through one total function over (intent × book × side), so there is no book state in which the desk's sentence has undefined meaning. This is the table, exactly as shipped and exactly as tested cell for cell:

IntentSpot book (long)Futures, longFutures, shortFlat
open/add (bull)buybuycover full, then buybuy
open/add (bear)→ hold (no spot short)sell (reduces toward the view)shortshort (futures) / hold (spot)
reducesell × frac of booksell × frac of bookcover × frac of bookno-op, journalled
closesell fullsell fullcover fullno-op, journalled
flipsell full (degrades to close)sell full + shortcover full + buy→ open
hold / armno order; arm stores an AI-chosen trigger the 60s control scan will obey (phase 3)

Why the asymmetry is the entire safety argument.

Authority moved to the AI, but only in the direction where a mistake is bounded. When the desk asks to take risk it can be clamped and refused by nine gates. When it asks to shed risk, nothing on the platform is allowed to say no — a reduce can be grown (a dust slice becomes the $1 minimum) but never shrunk, and a close passes every gate configuration including the confidence floor. Production proof below: a close at confidence 0.10 against a 0.99 floor flattened the book, and an intent riding on a hold verb still closed.

Two honest findings from the same session.

① The house-default bracket sits exactly ON the R:R floor. Two QA opens were refused with reward 0.1082 < 2× risk 0.0541 — the default 5%/10% bracket computes reward = 2.0000× risk and float rounding decides which side of the strict < it lands on. Pre-existing (not Plan 17), intermittent by entry price, worth a one-line fix (< → a small epsilon).

② Live-model intent compliance is partial. The local gpt-oss:20b emitted a valid intent on roughly half the observed real cycles and no parseable JSON at all on some. The pipeline is proven; the shadow dataset's growth rate depends on the model, not the machinery.
