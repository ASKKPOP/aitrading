Plan 17 — AI Order Authority

Replacing programmed buy / sell / clear logic with orders the sooppiy.com AI authors itself. Algorithm, flow, and diagrams.

Written 2026-07-29. The central claim in §2 is arithmetic, reproduced to the cent against four real production exits. Ships to prod sooppiy.com per standing rule.

요약 (Korean summary)

“모든 경우를 프로그래밍 로직으로 판단하는 건 정말 어렵다”는 진단이 정확합니다. 다만 문제는 로직이 복잡해서가 아니고, AI가 주문의 여섯 요소 중 하나 반만 결정하고 있다는 데 있습니다. 특히 수량은 AI가 전혀 정하지 않습니다. 2026-07-29 프로덕션 청산 4건을 검증한 결과, 청산 수량은 예외 없이 max_bet × adapt_배수 × conviction_배수 × 레버리지 ÷ 가격이었고 네 건 모두 센트 단위까지 정확히 일치했습니다. 그 공식의 어느 항도 “지금 들고 있는 포지션”을 참조하지 않습니다. 818이 17%가 아니라 39%를 청산한 이유는 판단이 바뀐 게 아니라 직전 청산이 이익이었기 때문입니다. 게다가 3배 레버리지에서 conviction 항은 conviction > 0.614가 아니면 항상 하한 0.30에 고정되어, 실측 conviction(0.365~0.5)에서는 아무 역할도 하지 않습니다. 또 데스크에는 “전량 청산”이라는 동사가 아예 없습니다. 해결책은 3개 동사 스칼라를 Order Intent 객체로 바꾸고, 기존 게이트를 전부 레일로 남기되 비대칭으로 두는 것입니다: 진입은 축소만, 청산은 확대만.

1. What the desk actually decides today

Six things determine an order. The AI authors one of them outright and half of another.

Order componentDecided byWhere
Direction — one of buy | sell | holdAIrun_engine.py:2083 (the JSON contract)

One clarification, because I assumed the opposite before checking: risk_engine.evaluate_order is side-aware. sell and cover short-circuit at line 423 and return the requested quantity untouched — Kelly, vol-sizing, and the exposure caps never see an exit. That is correct behaviour and it makes the finding below cleaner, because it means the exit quantity is a pure product of four constants with nothing else intervening.

2. The proof: nobody chose 17% or 39%

On 2026-07-29 the desk took four profit-banking actions — the first desk-initiated exits in the platform’s history. They reduced by 24.9%, 66.3%, 16.5%, and 39.3% of the position. Those look like judgements. They are not. Every one is:

quantity = min( position ,  max_bet_usd × adapt_size_mult × conviction_scale × leverage / price )

Not one term refers to the position being exited. Here is the reproduction, against the real signals rows and the real config_snapshot_json:

SigRunSidemax_bet× adapt× conv× L= predicted $actual $% of position
174811 ETHsell2,427.740.250.303546.24546.24 exact24.9%
177811 ETHsell2,427.740.500.3031,092.481,092.48 exact66.3%
175818 MNQcover2,225.430.250.303500.72500.72 exact16.5%
178818 MNQcover2,225.430.500.3031,001.441,001.44 exact39.3%

Four for four, to the cent. Two consequences follow, and both are worse than “the sizing is arbitrary”.

(a) 818 covered 39.3% rather than 16.5% because its previous cover made money.

The only term that changed between signal 175 and signal 178 is adapt_size_mult, which the adapt ladder doubled from 0.25 to 0.50 after recording "consecutive_wins": 1. The desk’s reading of the position did not enter into it. A win-streak multiplier designed to size entries is silently deciding how much of a winner to bank.

(b) The conviction term contributed nothing — it was pinned at its floor all four times.

_conviction_size_scale (run_engine.py:1965–1986) computes base = max(0.40, (v−0.45)/0.30) and then, at leverage L with damping 0.5, raises it to the power 1 + 0.5(L−1) with a floor of 0.30. At L = 3 that is max(0.30, base²), so the scale only leaves 0.30 once base > 0.548, i.e. conviction > 0.614. Observed convictions on these runs sit around 0.365–0.50. So on the extreme tier — which all three live runs use — Plan 15 rec-2’s stated goal that “bet size follows stated conviction” is inert. It is a constant 0.30.

This is the concrete form of “it’s really hard to make decisions that cover all the cases”. The logic is not too complex; it is answering a different question than the one being asked. Entry sizing asks how much risk should I take? An exit asks how much of what I hold should I give up? Those have different units, and the code uses the first to answer the second.

3. Four things the desk cannot say

The sentenceWhy it is unreachableObserved cost
“Close it.”No verb. The action enum the desk may emit is buy | sell | hold; flattening happens only when _evaluate_stop returns a reason and sets stop_flatten.Run 776 (ZC) peaked at +$78.58 and was retired by a rail at +$4.64 — 94% given back. The desk emitted buy for 43 straight cycles because “get out” was not in its vocabulary.
“Take half off.”No quantity authority on exits — §2.The four exits above. 39% instead of 17% for reasons unconnected to the position.
“I was wrong — reverse.”A flip needs two cycles 30–60 min apart, and the second is likely blocked: run_feedback.blocked_reentry exists precisely to stop same-direction re-entry after a losing close (run_engine.py:2411).Not yet quantified — but structurally the desk cannot act on being wrong within one cycle.
“Hold — and here is the level that changes my mind.”hold carries no state. Next cycle re-derives from scratch. There is no way to arm a conditional between cycles.Every between-cycle move is unattended for 30–60 min. The trailing lock is the only thing watching, and it arms at trail_activate_rr = 1.0 × max_loss, which for run 818 meant $89.02 against a $35.16 peak — unreachable.

4. The algorithm — Order Intent

Replace the 3-verb scalar with one object. Keep every existing gate, as a rail on that object rather than as the author of it.

{
  "intent":     "open" | "add" | "reduce" | "close" | "flip" | "hold" | "arm",
  "size_frac":  0.0-1.0,
  "size_basis": "budget" | "position",     // set by intent, not by the model
  "stop_pct":   number | null,
  "target_pct": number | null,
  "leverage":   number | null,             // may only reduce; Plan 16's add_margin
  "arm":        { "if": "price_below" | "price_above" | "health_below",
                  "level": number,
                  "then": "reduce" | "close",
                  "size_frac": 0.0-1.0 },
  "confidence": 0.0-1.0,
  "scores":     { thesis_alignment, momentum_quality, entry_quality, risk_context },
  "why":        "one sentence"
}

Design rule 1 — the basis switches with the intent

Intentsize_frac is a fraction of…Gate stack
open, addmax_bet_usd — risk being takenfull entry stack: fee_edge, confidence rank, add-churn, budget headroom, Kelly / vol / exposure, dust floor, R:R floor
reduce, closethe position the desk actually holds. close ≡ reduce at 1.0.exit stack only: market open, mark fresh, proceeds clear the round-trip fee on the slice. Nothing else.
flipdecomposes to close(1.0) + open(size_frac) in one transactionclose half unrailed; open half faces the full entry stack
armfraction of position, evaluated later by the ~60s control scanmay only ever reduce; never opens anything

Design rule 2 — the two strongest ideas here

arm is the answer to the between-cycles gap, and it needs no rail to pick a level.

arm lets the desk make a decision now that executes later, at machine latency, with the AI choosing the trigger and the machine merely obeying it. This is the shape the standing rule asks for and the shape four measured threshold experiments failed to produce: trail_activate_rr, a breakeven ratchet, a peak-relative brake cap, and RUN_CONF_V2 were each tested against real tick history and each rejected. The reason they failed is the same every time — one constant cannot be right for MNQ at 3× and USDZAR at 2× simultaneously. arm deletes the constant and puts a per-position judgement in its place. Note it must be stored through validate_stop_conditions, which strips unknown keys: adding a field without whitelisting it there is a silent no-op.

leverage as a reducible field is Plan 16’s add_margin.

Deleveraging moves the liquidation price away without surrendering any of the position. A desk that believes its thesis and is merely being squeezed should usually prefer it to reducing. Today leverage is chosen once by the tier table and never revisited, so this option does not exist at any point in a position’s life.

5. Rails, and why they must be asymmetric

order_params.py already establishes the right pattern — _SHRINK_ONLY, absolute bounds, journalled either way, shadow by default. Generalise it, with one change that carries the entire safety argument:

FieldOn an open / addOn a reduce / closeRationale
size_fracclamps DOWN onlyclamps UP onlyThe asymmetry is the point. A desk asking to take more risk may be refused. A desk asking to shed risk may always shed more, never less.
closen/anever railedThere is no safe reason to refuse a flatten. Refusing one is how a rail becomes the risk.
leverageclamps DOWN to the tier capUnchanged from order_params.
stop_pct / target_pctstop tightens only; R:R floor appliesUnchanged from order_params. The R:R floor already refuses a bracket that risks more than it can win, whoever authored it.
arm.levelnot railed — but arm.then may only reduceThe level is the judgement. Railing it reintroduces the constant we are trying to delete.
flipthe open half faces every entry gateA flip the gates would refuse degrades to a plain close — the correct failure mode, since the desk’s stated view is that the old position is wrong.

6. The compiler

The ledger’s vocabulary does not change: EXECUTABLE_ACTIONS = ("buy", "sell", "short", "cover") stays exactly as it is (automation.py:40). Intents compile to it. One function, one place, tested per book type — because this is precisely where the run-776 class of bug lives.

IntentSpot bookFutures book, longFutures book, shortFlat
open (bull)buybuycover then buybuy
open (bear)n/a → holdsellshortshort
reducesell × fracsell × fraccover × fracno-op
closesell fullsell fullcover fullno-op
flipsell full no short on spotsell full + shortcover full + buy→ open

The futures remap currently sitting inline in run_cycle (lines 2387–2394) moves into this table. That remap is a special case bolted into the cycle body; as a compiler it becomes a total function over (intent × book × side) that can be exhaustively tested. Run 776’s failure — a futures long told only short mechanics — was possible because no such total function existed.

7. The prompt

Both decision paths need this, not just the cheap one. About 40 of 43 cycles on run 776 routed to light, so _light_analysis is the production decision path — but the E1 router escalates to full for position reasons, and PR #125 had to fix exactly that blind spot once already. Shipping this to one path only would repeat that mistake.

Your book: 0.0928 units (negative = short, 0 = flat).
Open P&L on this position: +$12.22. Best this run has reached: $35.16 —
you are holding 35% of that high-water mark.

Choose ONE intent:
  open   - take a new position          size_frac = fraction of your max bet
  add    - increase the existing one    size_frac = fraction of your max bet
  reduce - bank part of it              size_frac = fraction of WHAT YOU HOLD
  close  - out entirely
  flip   - you were wrong; reverse      size_frac sizes the new side
  hold   - do nothing this cycle
  arm    - do nothing NOW, but set a level that acts without waiting for
           the next cycle:  {"if":"price_above","level":27800,
                             "then":"reduce","size_frac":0.5}

size_frac on reduce/close is a fraction of YOUR CURRENT POSITION, not of
your budget. 0.5 means half of what you hold. Say what you mean; the
platform will not second-guess a reduction.

Banking is a first-class action, not merely the absence of an add.
If you choose hold, name the level that would change your mind — and
prefer 'arm' over 'hold' whenever you can name one, because your next
cycle is 45 minutes away and the market is not.

That last paragraph is the whole design in prose. The current prompt’s hold is a dead end; arm turns it into a decision with a deadline the desk sets itself.

8. Rollout with real measurement

Mirror order_params exactly: RUN_ORDER_INTENT = off | shadow | apply, default shadow, intent journalled beside the legacy decision either way. Unlike a threshold change, most of this is measurable in shadow, which is the strongest argument for doing it this way:

IntentShadow-measurable?How
reduceyes, cleanlyFor every shadow reduce 0.50, compute what banking 50% would have realised at that mark and compare with what the machine’s entry-sized reduce actually banked. A true paired A/B with nothing at risk.
add, openyesSame pairing on size. The existing order_params shadow dataset already collects the bracket half of this.
closepartlyWe know what the remainder did afterwards, so a retrospective comparison is possible — but it is a path-not-taken, and the same lookahead trap that invalidated my first trail_activate_rr replay applies. Tick-by-tick only, no full-life peaks.
flip, armnoTheir counterfactual is a position that never existed. The only honest evidence is a live, small-budget cohort. Say so rather than manufacturing a number.

Promotion criteria to apply: ≥ 30 paired reduce observations, and the shadow intent ≥ the machine on median banked-per-exit. Phase order:

  1. reduce + close + the exit-gate split. Exits first — they can only shed risk, so this is the lowest-risk half and it is where §2’s defect lives.
  2. open/add with size_basis: budget. Subsumes order_params.size_frac.
  3. arm. Needs validate_stop_conditions whitelisting and a control-scan evaluator; test through the API, not the planner.
  4. flip, and the blocked_reentry interaction — a flip must not be mistaken for a whipsaw re-entry by the guard that exists to stop whipsaws.
  5. leverage reduction, jointly with Plan 16 phase 4.

Verification

  1. Compiler exhaustiveness: a test per cell of the §6 table — 7 intents × 3 book states × 2 market types. Run 776 proves an untested cell is a live bug.
  2. The asymmetry: assert a reduce can be railed up but never down, and that close passes every gate configuration including a tripped auto-breaker (risk_engine.py:423–427 already guarantees this for the ledger; the intent layer must not undo it).
  3. Regression on §2: a test that fails if an exit quantity is ever derived from max_bet_usd. This is the defect; it deserves a permanent tripwire.
  4. Mutation: re-inject each fix — restore the entry-sizing exit, drop the basis switch, remove close from the enum — and confirm a test fails for each. Clear __pycache__ in the runner.
  5. Backward compatibility: a decision with no intent key must compile from the legacy verb, so the fallback path ("light cycle: no LLM verdict") and any older model keep working unchanged.

9. What breaks

  • The adapt ladder’s role narrows. Once exits are position-based, adapt_size_mult stops influencing them. That is the intended fix, but run_feedback’s own accounting assumes it sizes everything, so its tests need revisiting alongside.
  • _add_limit_skip must not see reductions. The add-churn guard gates ("buy", "sell", "short") today (run_engine.py:2483) — and sell is in that list because on a futures book it may be an open. Once intent distinguishes add from reduce, the guard keys on the intent and the ambiguity disappears. This is the same class of bug PR #118 fixed, in reverse.
  • Telemetry shape changes. The decision event gains intent/size_frac/size_basis. The Automation UI reads these events; adding fields is safe, but the four summary tiles on AutomationPage.tsx assume the current shape.
  • What this does not fix. Intent authority does not make the desk’s judgement better — it makes its judgement expressible. Run 811 banked at roughly +$6.45 after holding through +$33.44, which is capability without calibration. Giving it reduce 0.5 lets it act on a view; it does not supply the view. Calibration is a separate problem and should not be claimed as a benefit of this change.
한국어 상세

진단: 문제는 “프로그래밍 로직이 모든 경우를 다루기 어렵다”가 아니라, 그 로직이 다른 질문에 답하고 있다는 것입니다. 진입 사이징은 “얼마나 리스크를 질까”를 묻고, 청산은 “들고 있는 것 중 얼마를 놓을까”를 묻습니다. 단위가 다른데 코드는 전자로 후자에 답합니다. 프로덕션 청산 4건이 max_bet × adapt × conviction × L로 센트까지 정확히 재현됩니다 — 어느 항도 보유 포지션을 보지 않습니다.

두 가지 나쁜 결과: (a) 818이 39%를 청산한 건 판단이 아니라 직전 청산이 이익이어서 adapt 배수가 0.25→0.50으로 두 배가 된 결과입니다. (b) 3배 레버리지에서 conviction 항은 conviction > 0.614가 아니면 하한 0.30에 고정 — 실측 0.365~0.5이므로 “확신에 따라 베팅 크기가 따라간다”는 Plan 15의 목표가 extreme 등급에서는 작동하지 않습니다.

설계: 3동사 스칼라 → Order Intent 객체. open/add/reduce/close/flip/hold/arm. 핵심 규칙 두 개: ① size_frac의 기준이 intent에 따라 바뀝니다 — 진입은 예산 대비, 청산은 보유 포지션 대비. ② 레일이 비대칭입니다 — 진입은 줄이는 방향으로만, 청산은 늘리는 방향으로만, close는 절대 막지 않습니다. 리스크를 줄이겠다는 요청을 거부하는 레일은 그 자체가 리스크입니다.

가장 중요한 새 동사는 arm입니다. “지금은 아무것도 안 하지만 이 가격이 오면 절반 청산” — AI가 수준을 정하고 기계는 60초 스캔으로 복종만 합니다. 상수 4개를 실제 틱 데이터로 측정해 전부 기각했던 이유가 “하나의 상수가 MNQ 3배와 USDZAR 2배에 동시에 맞을 수 없다”였습니다. arm은 상수를 없애고 그 자리에 포지션별 판단을 넣습니다.

측정 계획: reduce는 섀도우에서 완전한 페어드 A/B가 가능합니다(같은 시점에 50% 청산했으면 얼마였는지 대 기계가 실제로 청산한 금액). close는 부분적으로만, flip·arm은 섀도우로 측정 불가 — 소액 실거래 코호트만이 정직한 증거입니다. 없는 숫자를 만들지 않겠습니다.

과장하지 않을 것: 이 변경은 데스크의 판단을 좋게 만들지 않습니다. 표현 가능하게 만듭니다. 811은 +$33.44를 지나 +$6.45에 청산했습니다 — 능력은 있고 캘리브레이션은 없는 상태입니다. 캘리브레이션은 별개 문제이며 이 변경의 효과로 주장하지 않겠습니다.

Implementation status — 2026-07-30

  • Built (PR #136): order_intent.py — parse, the asymmetric rails, and the §6 compiler as a total function tested cell for cell; the prompt on both decision paths; RUN_ORDER_INTENT = off|shadow|apply defaulting to shadow, journalling intent_size_usd beside machine_size_usd on every cycle (the §8 paired reduce dataset); apply executes exits only (phase 1). The §2 defect carries a permanent end-to-end tripwire — a test fails if an exit is ever sized from max_bet_usd again.
  • Two deviations from this doc, both narrowing: ① the light path asks for the intent only when a position exists — phase 1 is exits, a flat book’s only intents are open/hold which the verb already expresses, and the cheap path stays cheap (its token budget still rose 200→280 so the intent object cannot truncate the JSON). ② add compiles identically to open — the §6 table has no add row, and both face the same basis and the same entry stack, so a separate row would be a distinction without a difference.
  • §9’s add-churn worry resolved itself by construction: an intent exit compiles to sell only against a long — which _add_limit_skip’s own qty/action matrix already reads as a reduce and never limits — and to cover against a short, which is not in the gated action list at all. The futures-short sell ambiguity is resolved by compilation; no exemption was needed, and an exemption written first was deleted as dead code.
  • Not built, per the phase order: arm storage/evaluation (parsed, validated and journalled only — needs the validate_stop_conditions whitelist and a control-scan evaluator), flip execution and its blocked_reentry interaction, leverage reduction (joint with Plan 16 phase 4), and the open/add apply phase. Each is gated on shadow evidence: ≥30 paired reduce observations with the shadow intent ≥ the machine on median banked-per-exit.
